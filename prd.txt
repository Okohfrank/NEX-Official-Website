NEX — Network of Engineering Xcellence
Website Product Requirements Document (PRD)
Prepared for: Development Team (President + Sam Charles)
Goal: A world-class, student-oriented platform supporting NEX's full research-to-development pipeline.
1. Overview & Product Vision
NEX's website is not a brochure site — it is the operating system for the society. It should take a member from first sign-up through problem research, team-based development, and public publication of their work, while feeling as engaging and well-designed as the best modern student/creator platforms.
Design principles to hold throughout:
Welcoming — low-friction entry, clear next steps, never intimidating for a first-year student
Competitive — visible progress, rankings, and recognition that feel earned
Addictive to real output — engagement loops should reward research, building, and documentation, not passive scrolling
Credible — should look and feel like something a company or research institute would trust enough to partner with
2. Core User Journey
The site should be structured around this end-to-end flow:
Discover NEX (public landing) → Register → Get Placed in a Subgroup → Lock a Research Topic → Research Workspace → Development/Build Workspace → Documentation & Publication → Recognition (points, badges, leaderboard)
3. Feature List
3.1 Public Landing / Marketing Layer
Homepage: mission, vision, focus areas, and live stats (member count, active projects, missions completed)
Public project showcase — visible to non-members; this is NEX's credibility and PR asset
Partnerships & sponsors page
Publications / annual reports archive
Clean, modern visual identity — this is often the first thing a partner org or sponsor sees
3.2 Executives Section
A public-facing page/section showcasing NEX's leadership team, designed to build credibility with prospective members, partners, and sponsors.
Founder Feature: a dedicated founder spotlight at the top of the section, separate from the executive team — photo, name, credentials (academic background, honors/awards), and a short narrative on how NEX was conceived and brought to life.
Executive Team Grid: each executive displayed with photo, full name, role/title, department, and a short bio (2–3 sentences highlighting relevant background, skills, or achievements).
Profiles displayed in hierarchical order (President down to Finance Officer), consistent with the organization's structure
Each profile expandable or linkable to a fuller bio page, optionally including a short message/quote from that executive
Optional: link out to each executive's LinkedIn or portfolio, reinforcing NEX's credibility
Design notes: uniform photo framing/card layout since this page is often what a partner or sponsor evaluates first; fully mobile-responsive; easy for the Head of Digital Infrastructure to update as executives change each academic year.
3.3 Registration & Identity
Sign-up fields: matric number, institutional email, department, level, skills, area of strongest knowledge, subgroup interest
Institutional email verification (confirms real student, reduces duplicate/fake accounts)
Auto-generated member profile page — becomes a living portfolio over time
3.4 Onboarding & Placement
Subgroup selection based on focus areas (e.g. Water Purity, Waste & Recycling, Smart Agriculture, Energy, Lab Tools, Digital/AI, Infrastructure)
Tiered access model (recommended): everyone who registers gets full access to explore, learn, and attend workshops immediately. Placement onto an active project team is the competitive step — based on registration data, skills, and demonstrated engagement. This avoids outright rejecting new members (which can permanently drive away first-time joiners for a young, unproven society) while still keeping team placement meaningful.
3.5 Research Module
This is the core of the platform — the pipeline from studying a problem to building a solution.
Topic Lock-In: each team selects/submits a semester research topic. Once submitted, the topic is locked — changeable at most once, and only with a submitted reason, reviewed by the Technical & Research Lead.
Guided Research Workspace: a structured template (not a blank page) walking teams through Problem Identification → Background/Literature Research → Findings, so first-time researchers aren't lost.
Research Log: timestamped progress updates visible to the team and execs — keeps momentum visible, not just a final dump at the end.
Mentor Review: the Technical & Research Lead (or assigned reviewer) can leave feedback and approve progression to the next stage.
3.6 Development / Build Module
Unlocks once the research phase is marked complete by a reviewer
Build log: photos, code repositories, design files (CAD, PCB, etc.), iteration notes
Skill-tagging per contribution (e.g. 'embedded systems', 'data analysis', 'CAD design') — feeds the scoring system
Structured Testing & Impact Assessment fields — guided, not freeform, so it reliably gets completed
3.7 Documentation & Publication
Auto-compiled final report template — pulls research, build, and testing sections into one document
One-click publish to the public showcase (with team consent) — becomes part of NEX's institutional memory and each member's public portfolio
3.8 Team Matching Engine
Skill-diverse matching: when forming project teams, the algorithm ensures no more than one member with a given primary skill lands in the same group — communicated to members as intentional design for diverse, well-rounded teams, not blind randomness.
Admin override — execs can manually adjust matches before finalizing; the algorithm should assist, not replace human judgment
3.9 Scoring & Recognition System
Skill-weighted project scoring: at project completion, the system (with exec input) identifies which skill contributed most to the outcome and awards extra points to that skill's contributor.
To keep this fair, score across multiple axes rather than one dominant metric:
Technical contribution
Documentation quality
Collaboration / peer nomination
Initiative and consistency (from the research log activity)
Leaderboard — semester-based, resetting each term so new joiners aren't permanently behind
Skill badges — collectible, visual, shareable (e.g. 'Water Systems Specialist', 'AI Contributor')
Auto-generated certificates (PDF) for completed workshops and projects — CV-ready
Permanent 'Founding Cohort' badge for this launch group, independent of later ranking
3.10 Community & Engagement Tools
Problem Bounty Board — members submit real problems they've spotted; credited on submission; feeds future Mission topics
Events/workshops calendar with RSVP
Announcements/news feed
Member directory searchable by skill/subgroup — helps organic collaboration beyond assigned teams
3.11 Admin / Executive Dashboard
View all teams, research status, and submission stages at a glance
Approve topic-change requests, review submissions, manage placements
Analytics: engagement levels, most active members, subgroup distribution, drop-off points
4. World-Class Extras to Consider
Features seen on top-tier platforms (open-source communities, YC/Product Hunt-style sites, LinkedIn-adjacent portfolio tools) that would elevate NEX beyond a typical student-society site:
Public API / open data for NEX's published research — signals credibility to academic and industry partners.
Contribution graph (GitHub-style activity heatmap) on each member profile — visually shows consistency over time, not just total points.
Mentor/Alumni layer — once NEX has graduates, give them lightweight accounts to review projects and mentor, keeping talent connected post-graduation.
Shareable project pages with OG image cards — so a project link posted on LinkedIn/Twitter renders a clean preview card automatically.
Version history on research/build submissions — so a topic's evolution is preserved, not overwritten (good for real documentation integrity, echoes IP protection instincts).
Notification system (email/WhatsApp integration) for review feedback, deadlines, and leaderboard changes — keeps members engaged without needing to check the site manually.
Accessibility & mobile-first design — most members will access this primarily from phones; the entire experience should be excellent on mobile, not just responsive.
Dark mode — small detail, but expected on modern platforms and cheap to implement early.
5. Build Sequencing Recommendation
Given a one-week build timeline with two developers, sequence features to get members active fast rather than waiting for a fully complete platform:
MVP (Week 1 priority): registration & profiles, subgroup selection, basic research workspace (topic lock-in + guided template), public landing page, basic leaderboard.
Phase 2 (post-launch, iterate live): team matching engine, full scoring system, admin dashboard analytics, badges/certificates, problem bounty board, mentor/alumni layer, contribution graphs.
Shipping the MVP fast and iterating in public keeps the WhatsApp-first momentum going rather than delaying member activity for a 'complete' launch.
6. Non-Functional Requirements
Fast load times — most members will be on mobile data, not campus wifi
Secure handling of matric numbers and institutional emails (basic data protection hygiene)
Scalable data model — subgroups, teams, and focus areas will expand as NEX grows to more departments and potentially other universities
Clean, documented codebase — future dev leads (post-graduation handover) will need to maintain this
